Timoteo Dinelli

/tiˈmɔteo diˈnɛlli/

"Sempre forte, con coraggio e senza paura". 

I am a postdoctoral researcher at Stanford University, working in the [FxLAB], supervised by Prof. Matthias Ihme. I received my Ph.D. in Chemical Engineering from Politecnico di Milano, where I was part of the [CRECK Modeling Laboratory] under the supervision of Prof. Alessandro Stagni.

My research applies data-driven methods to chemical kinetics, which is a fancy way of saying I teach computers to predict how things burn. I build frameworks that combine experimental and synthetic measurements with models, producing predictive, uncertainty-aware models of combustion and pyrolysis for both conventional and renewable fuels.

Along the way, I've worked on chemical lumping, automated validation, optimization and reduction strategies that keep simulations affordable. Next, I'm taking these tools to supercritical fluids and ultrafast chemistry. I enjoy writing code, and lately I have been obsessed with automatic differentiation. Derivatives tell you which knobs actually matter. I like knowing which knobs matter.

As you can see, my interests are terribly broad. I admit my exuberance, and I do nothing to limit it. Somehow I get to chase diverse and interesting questions while also holding down a paying job (see my [cv] for proof that I occasionally focus).
